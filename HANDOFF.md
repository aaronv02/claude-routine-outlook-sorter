# Your email, sorted

One page. Nothing here can break your mailbox.

---

## What it does

It puts **coloured labels** on your mail — Grants, Donors & Gifts, Board &
Governance, Events, and so on — so you can see at a glance what's in front of you.

It does **not** move, file, archive, or delete anything. Every message stays exactly
where it is. The labels are tags, and you can change or remove any of them whenever
you like.

It runs by itself, about once an hour. There's nothing to open and no button to
press.

---

## The one thing worth knowing

**If a label is wrong, just change it in Outlook the way you normally would.**
Right-click the message, pick the category you actually wanted.

That's it. That's the whole interface. Next time it runs, it notices you disagreed
and learns from it:

- That sender is now permanently correct. It will never get that one wrong again.
- Similar mail from *new* senders gets better too.

So the first week or two you'll be correcting it a fair bit, and then it quiets down.
That's the design working, not a fault.

**Anything it isn't confident about gets labelled `⚠ Needs Review`** rather than
guessed at. A wrong label is worse than an honest "I don't know."

---

## It gets faster, and eventually runs itself

Once you've corrected a sender a few times, it hands that sender over to **Outlook's
own rules**. After that, mail from them gets labelled the instant it arrives — even
if everything else here stopped running tomorrow.

You'll find those under Outlook's own Rules settings, named `Inbox Steward: …`. They
are ordinary Outlook rules and you can delete any of them yourself.

## Just ask Claude

This is the part you'll probably use most. Open Claude and ask in plain English —
there are no commands to learn.

**Catching up**

- *"What emails am I forgetting?"*
- *"Who is waiting on me?"*
- *"Anything I've left hanging more than a week?"*
- *"Catch me up, I was out Tuesday and Wednesday."*
- *"How was my week?"*

**Finding things**

- *"Anything from Tessa this month?"*
- *"What did I tell the board about the audit?"*
- *"Did I ever reply to that bequest question?"*
- *"Show me unread mail about grants."*

**Looking ahead**

- *"What's coming up next week?"*
- *"Any meetings I haven't responded to?"*
- *"How many hours am I booked next week?"*

**Sorting, together**

- *"Suggest labels for my recent mail"* — it proposes, you look, and nothing is
  applied until you say so.

If Claude says it can't see your email, it needs to be fully quit and reopened —
right-click its icon near the clock, choose Quit, then open it again. Closing the
window isn't enough.

---

## Fridays

You'll get a summary of the week: what's still waiting on a reply, what you flagged,
what went unread, invitations you haven't answered, and what's coming.

One honest note about the "your week" part of it: Outlook can only see that a meeting
existed and that mail was sent. It can't see what was decided, whether you actually
attended, or anything that happened on the phone, on a site visit, or in another
system. Read it as a record of activity, not of what you accomplished.

---

## If something looks wrong

- **Labels aren't appearing.** Tell whoever set this up — a sign-in may have expired.
- **Everything says Needs Review.** Same.
- **A label is wrong.** Change it in Outlook. That's the fix and the training in one
  action.
- **Something keeps landing in the wrong place.** Mention which and it can be tuned.

## To undo everything

- Delete the categories from Outlook's category list — that removes every label it
  applied.
- Delete the `Inbox Steward:` rules in Outlook's Rules settings — that stops the
  automatic labelling.

Nothing was ever moved or deleted, so there's nothing to put back.
